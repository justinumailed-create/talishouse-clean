1. Purpose and who we call

Why this playbook exists

On Sunday 1 November 2026, Ralf (the owner of the Tsplits™ concept, with 25 years in Canadian real estate) ran a coaching and mock-call session with the team: Arun, Enanko and Aritri. This playbook turns that session, Ralf’s review of the first draft and his Tsplits™ eBook into a guide you can follow on live calls.

It is a guide, not a script. Ralf was clear: “Not the words. If you’re reciting something, people are going to know. The moment that they know, they’re going to turn off.” Learn the points, then say them in your own words.

Who we call

  • Independent (non-franchised) real estate brokers. Brokers who run their own brokerage and are not part of a franchise network. In the eBook these are the people who become Root Account Partners: “Typically they are brokers or team leaders.”
  • Team leaders are also a fit, for the same reason.
  • Canada: Aritri. USA: Enanko.
  • Prospects come from the team’s CSV list. Each row shows the broker’s website, how many employees they have and what kind of listings they do.
  • Prospects have already replied to our text message outreach. Do not mention the text messages on the call. They know they responded.

What a good call achieves

The prospect hears the two lines, understands that the map site turns part of their advertising budget into income, and walks through the map site we have already built for them on that same call.

2. The Tsplits™ offer in plain words

Tsplits™ provides brokers with a map site: a map-based, industry-adjacent marketing platform that the broker controls and that can earn money for the broker.

  • Industry-adjacent means it sits next to the industry platforms (MLS®, brokerage websites), not inside them. It does not replace them. As the eBook FAQ says, map sites “can work alongside them to reach people otherwise often missed.” Kijiji and other classifieds are not industry platforms.
  • Revenue-producing means other people pay the broker to be on the map site. Buyers and sellers whose listings go on it pay the broker’s minimum commission and sign up for the broker’s minimum term length (the service floor). Local businesses pay for AdPro pins. In the eBook’s words, the broker “has turned this portion of an advertising strategy from an expense into a revenue opportunity that only gets better with increased adoption.”
  • The broker keeps all existing marketing. Ralf: “They don’t have to change what they’ve done all along one bit.” Tsplits™ simply introduces a part of the advertising budget that pays back.
  • Nobody else offers this. Ralf: “Kijiji can’t do that, TV can’t do that, radio can’t do that, brokerage websites can’t do that, MLS can’t do that, nobody can do that. We can do that for you.”
  • We are selling their product, not ours. Ralf: “We’re not selling our product, we’re selling their product.” The map site is the broker’s own platform, in the broker’s market, earning for the broker.

What Tsplits™ and the map site are not (from the eBook FAQ):

  • Not a real estate brokerage. The broker’s transactions are handled by professionals they choose.
  • Not a referral-fee business. “We charge to maintain accounts and provide services.”
  • Not a long-term commitment. “Simply let accounts or entries lapse and there is no further obligation” associated with them.

3. The two-line pitch and the hook

The hook (line 1)

“I would like to show you how you can make your advertising budget revenue-producing. Is that something you would like to explore?”

This should be the first thing you say after the greeting and the quick check that you have the right broker. The keywords are advertising budget and revenue-producing.

Do not open with “Good news”. Ralf: the good news is implicit, and said out loud it sounds a bit smug.

Do not open with pins, markers, eBooks, digital brochures or features. Ralf: “Their website does all of that, they don’t need us for that.”

The answer to “How?” (line 2)

The prospect will not believe you. Ralf: “Advertising is inherently an expense.” Expect “You’re kidding, right?” or “How can you do that?” Your answer:

“We provide industry-adjacent platforms we call map sites, entirely under your control.”

Then go straight to the map site you have built for them:

“In fact, we already set up a map site for you from a couple of your listings. Would you like to see it?”

Word rules for the pitch

Say Never say Why
“providing”, “we provide” “selling” or “offering” Ralf corrected both. “We are providing, not offering. We are providing solutions.”
“we call map sites” “like map site” A map site is our name for the platform, not a comparison.
“revenue-producing” “reduce your costs” The point is income, not savings.
“your map site”, “your market” “our product”, “our features” Every line should be about the prospect.

4. Pre-call research checklist

Ralf: “You adjust your argumentation to what you’ve already researched about them.” Do your research before you dial, not on the call. Information you gather is for the conversation; it is not a stage of the call. A quick AI search is enough for most of it.

Their local market

The four AdPro classes

  • Source service provider: the broker and their brokerage. Nothing happens until they sell something.
  • Primary service providers: mortgage brokers and lawyers. Necessary to close transactions.
  • Secondary service providers: inspectors, surveyors and similar. Not necessary, but advisable.
  • Ancillary service providers: anyone providing a service or product that links directly or indirectly to the real estate purchase, such as contractors, garden centres and electronics stores. More broadly, travel agencies, car dealerships and anything else that affects lifestyle or quality of life in the neighbourhood.

5. Call flow

Keep the call moving through five stages. The first two are fixed. Everything after that is conversation, and the goal is to do the walk-through on this call.

Stage 1: Opener (about 15 seconds)

  • Greet them by first name, give your name and say you are calling from Tsplits™.
  • Ask: “Is this a good time to talk?”
  • Confirm you have the broker: “You are the broker of Lakeside Realty, right?”
  • On confirmation, follow up: “Lakeside Realty is independent, right? You are not part of a franchise, are you?”
  • Do not mention the text messages, “your message”, or that they “showed interest”.

“Hi Sam, this is Enanko from Tsplits™. Is this a good time to talk? You are the broker of Lakeside Realty in Kingston, right?”

In these examples, Sam, Lakeside Realty and Kingston stand for the broker’s real name, brokerage and town.

Stage 2: The hook and the “how”

“Great, thank you. I would like to show you how you can make your advertising budget revenue-producing. Is that something you would like to explore?”

Pause. Let them react. When they ask how, or say it is impossible:

“We provide industry-adjacent platforms we call map sites. In fact, we already set up a map site for you from a couple of your listings. Would you like to see it?”

This is one of the reasons you open their website before you dial: you know two or three listings that work well on a map site, and you can cite them (“the one on Water Street in Kingston”).

Stage 3: The walk-through, right then and there

Do not stop to ask discovery questions. You already did your research before the call. Go straight to the walk-through.

  1. Open their map site first. Ralf: “The number one thing that you want to open up for these people is the map site that you’ve already built for them.” Give them the URL so they can follow along.
  2. Then open the Tsplits™ eBook and walk through it at a high level.
  3. Do the walk-through that day, on that call. Don’t give them time to change their mind or refocus.

“Let me quickly show you the map site we set up for you. It only takes a few minutes, and you can explore it to your heart’s content afterwards.”

Stage 4: Value points, between the lines

As you walk through, listen for what sits between the lines of what the broker says, and match it to one of these. Talk about their situation, not our features.

Between the lines… Bring up… What to say (in your own words)
“Getting full value for my services (commission) is hard.” Service floors protect your commission and term lengths Clients push agents from 5 or 6 percent down to 3 or 4 percent. Every time you take less than your minimum, you reduce what you earn, and the industry platforms give you no way to differentiate. On your map site, every buyer or seller who wants in pays your minimum and gives you a minimum term, whether that is 5 percent, 6 percent or, for some property classes, 10 percent. You set it. That also protects you from spending money to generate a sale when the term is realistically too short to succeed.
“Every agent here has the same tools.” Semi-exclusive, protected market We protect map site markets. They do not generally dilute into each other. In your market you would be the only one who has this listings-closing tool. If the word “semi” becomes an issue: a broker from a neighbouring market might place a PIN here for a listing legally under contract, just as you might place one there. That’s not dilution, that’s reciprocation.
“Good listings are hard to get.” Differentiation and top of mind Upscale homes, commercial properties, hotels, motels, campgrounds, lodges and chalets are hard to win. You need to be top of mind. The map site differentiates you, and nobody else has it. Map sites can move you up-market, keep you top of mind and generate word of mouth, the least expensive and most effective form of advertising, locally.
“I pay for advertising and see nothing back.” The budget becomes revenue-producing Keep Kijiji, social media, radio, TV and newspaper advertising. The map site is the only part of your advertising budget that becomes revenue-producing over time. None of those other channels can do that.
“I have (or had) an assistant.” Revenue-producing roles Ralf had an assistant for six and a half years at about $3,000 CAD a month. She did his legwork and brought in nothing. With a map site, that same role can earn: finding owners who have not listed yet, and local businesses that want to be referral partners or co-promoters with pins.
“I work with lawyers, inspectors, mortgage brokers.” AdPro pins for local businesses Every lawyer and mortgage broker you work with should have a pin on your map site. So should building inspectors and surveyors. People who buy a house tend to buy a car and book a holiday soon after, so travel agents and car dealers may want to be there too. A pin can start free, then become a co-promotion at, for example, $20 CAD a month.
“My area has tours and attractions.” Make the map site interesting Real estate on its own is not interesting to most people. What the area is like is. AdPro pins add life to the map site, drive traffic and pay you monthly or yearly.

Close every value point by bringing it back to the hook: “That’s how your advertising budget becomes revenue-producing.”

Stage 5: If they want more time

  1. Try to avoid booking a follow-up demo. The walk-through belongs on this call.
  2. If they insist on more time, agree a specific day and time in their time zone, and confirm who else should join (a partner, a team leader on their side, an assistant).
  3. Leave cost until they are sold. Ralf: “We don’t want to talk about cost as long as they’re not sold.” If they press, see “How much does it cost?” in section 6.

6. Objection handling

Ralf: “Over five to ten sales calls it all becomes second nature. People are always going to react the same way.” Answer only with the points below. Do not invent results, numbers or promises.

“You can’t make advertising revenue-producing. Advertising is an expense.” That is exactly why nobody else can do it. We provide you with an industry-adjacent platform, your map site, where other people may pay to be: listing clients pay your service floors in dollars and time, and local businesses may pay for AdPro pins. We have already set up a map site in your name. Would you like to explore that further?

“I already have a website.” Of course, I’ve seen it. It shows your listings well, and your map site does not replace it. The difference is, your website is an expense; your map site is the part of your advertising budget that makes you money and differentiates you.

“I already advertise on MLS®, Kijiji and social media.” Keep all of it. You don’t have to change anything you do today. The map site works alongside industry platforms, and it is the one piece of your advertising that can become revenue-producing.

“Is this another brokerage? Do you want a cut of my sales?” No. Tsplits™ is not a real estate brokerage. We do not charge referral fees. Your transactions stay with the professionals who work with you.

“I don’t want to be locked into a contract.” There is no long-term commitment. If you let your account lapse, our service stops and there is no further obligation.

“Won’t a service floor cost me listings?” You set your own floor. It protects you from working below your minimum and from becoming a discount broker.

“What if the broker down the road gets one too?” Map site markets are semi-exclusive. We protect map site markets, and they do not generally dilute into each other. In your market you would be the only one who has this listings-closing tool. If the word “semi” becomes an issue: a broker from a neighbouring market might place a PIN here for a listing legally under contract, just as you might place one there. That’s not dilution, that’s reciprocation.

“I don’t have time to sell advertising.” You don’t have to do it yourself. Your assistant or admin person can do it as part of their week, by providing pins to owners who have not listed yet and to local businesses. That turns a cost-only role into a revenue-producing one.

“Why would a local business pay to be on my map site?” Because your clients are their customers, too. People who buy a house need lawyers, inspectors and surveyors, for example. They also tend to renovate, buy a car and book a holiday soon after. Your AdPro pins can start free, and next year you can start a cost-sharing collective that actually makes you money.

“Send me some information.” Of course. The best information is the map site already built in your name. Can I show it to you now? Then you can explore it to your heart’s content.

“How much does it cost?” (Only if they press before they have seen the map site.) It depends on how many of your agents take part. Our aim is to show you that your service floors pay for the account many times over. Let me show you first, and then we can go through the numbers properly. If they have seen the map site and want figures, the eBook’s adoption section lists them: brokerage or team $1,000 a year or $100 a month, agent $200 a year or $100 a month, and $7 a week per listing PIN (“a dollar per day”). For the USA these are USD; for Canada they are CAD.

“Who is this? Why is someone from India calling me?” Stay calm and professional. Ralf: “Anybody who is being contacted by somebody from India expects to be sold something. We are not selling anything. We are providing solutions.” Go back to the hook: “I’m with Tsplits™, a Canadian company. I would like to show you how you can make your advertising budget revenue-producing.”

7. Do’s and don’ts

Do

  • Open with the hook: “I would like to show you how you can make your advertising budget revenue-producing.”
  • Follow with: “We provide industry-adjacent platforms we call map sites.”
  • Research the prospect, two or three of their listings and their local market before every call.
  • Do the walk-through on the first call: their map site first, then the eBook.
  • Make every sentence about the prospect. Ralf: “Is this outgoing or is it incoming for whom I’m talking to? If it’s incoming, it’s perfect.”
  • Provide solutions. Ralf: “In 25 years, I have never sold a piece of real estate. I’ve always provided solutions.”
  • Use your own personality and some playfulness, but stay professional.
  • Follow the points in this playbook, in your own words.
  • Confirm the prospect is independent, not a franchise broker.

Don’t

  • Don’t open with “Good news”. It sounds smug.
  • Don’t open with features: pins, markers, eBooks, digital brochures, listing showcases.
  • Don’t ask discovery questions you could have answered before the call.
  • Don’t mention the text message sequence, “your message” or “you showed interest”.
  • Don’t push for a follow-up booking. Walk through it now.
  • Don’t talk about cost until the prospect is sold.
  • Don’t say “selling” or “offering”. Say “providing”.
  • Don’t say “like map site”. Say “we call map sites”.
  • Don’t recite a script. Prospects can tell, and they switch off.
  • Don’t use emojis in any follow-up message.
  • Don’t invent figures, results or testimonials.
  • Don’t present the map site as a replacement for MLS® or their website.

8. Mock-call practice drills and scoring rubric

What went wrong in the 1 November mock calls

Caller What happened Lesson
Enanko (first attempt) Introduced Tsplits™ as “a real estate and marketing platform”, then explained map sites, listing markers, links and digital brochures. Ralf stopped the pitch. Feature-first opening. The prospect’s website already does all of that. Lead with the hook.
Arun (demonstration call) Good rapport, and confirmed the prospect was an independent broker. Then moved to showcasing listings, eBooks, agent pins and a Kijiji comparison. Ralf: “I still haven’t heard the hook.” Also referred to “your message”. The hook must come in the first, second or third sentence. Never refer to the text messages.
Aritri Opened with “you’ve shown interest in our map site”, lost her thread and said “reduce your revenue” when she meant costs. Ralf stopped her: “The two main messages, you haven’t got to.” Don’t reference the outreach. Deliver the two lines early, then talk.
Team drill Answered “how do we do that?” with “by selling map sites”, then “like map site”, then “we are offering solutions”. Providing, not selling or offering. “We call map sites.”

Drill 1: The two lines, cold

Partner says “Go.” You deliver both lines within 20 seconds of “Hello”, with no notes. Repeat until all three callers can do it five times in a row without a slip on “providing” or “we call map sites”.

Drill 2: “You’re kidding, right?”

Partner plays a sceptical broker and challenges the hook (“Advertising is an expense”, “Nobody can do that”). You answer with line 2 and offer to show their map site. No features allowed.

Drill 3: Five-minute research

Take a real prospect from the CSV. In five minutes, find two or three listings that would work well on a map site (street and town only), the number of agents, any paid administrators, and the market size. Then cite one listing in a sentence to your partner: “We already set up a map site for you from your listing on Water Street.”

Drill 4: Walk-through now

Partner plays a broker who tries to postpone (“Send me something”, “Call me next week”). You steer to a walk-through of their map site and eBook on this call, without pressure. Score it only if the walk-through starts.

Drill 5: Objection round

Partner fires five objections from section 6 in a random order. Answer each in two or three sentences using only the value points. Score one point for each answer with no invented claim.

Drill 6: Incoming or outgoing?

Record a two-minute call. Play it back and mark every sentence as “incoming” (about the prospect) or “outgoing” (about us). Rewrite the outgoing ones.

Scoring rubric

Score each mock call out of 20. Each line is scored 0 (missing), 1 (partly) or 2 (done well).

9. Glossary

Term Meaning
Map site (Mapsite) “A map-based real estate industry adjacent marketing platform provided by TSPLITS Inc. of Nova Scotia, Canada.” (eBook legend)
Industry-adjacent Sits next to the industry platforms (MLS®, brokerage websites) rather than inside them, so it is not limited by their rules.
Root Account Partner Usually a broker or team leader. Registers 50 miles (USA) or 80 km (Canada) around their Home PIN and grows one Root Mapsite. This is who we call.
Root Mapsite The broker’s own map site. The broker’s agents place PINs on it for their listings. Buyers and sellers whose listings appear on it pay the broker’s service floor, and the broker charges under SPLITS terms and per PIN.
Derivative Account Partner Usually a real estate professional (agent) who places PINs on a Root Mapsite. The Root Account Partner decides how many PINs each may place.
AdPro Account Partner Someone who builds a presence for exposure or profit with a single-PIN map site. In practice: local businesses and service providers who pay for a pin on the broker’s map site as referral partners or co-promoters.
AdPro classes Source: the broker and their brokerage. Primary: mortgage brokers and lawyers (necessary to close). Secondary: inspectors and surveyors (advisable, not necessary). Ancillary: contractors, garden centres, travel agencies, car dealerships and other businesses linked to the purchase or to life in the neighbourhood.
FSBO For-Sale-By-Owner sellers. They want to save on fees or sell anonymously, use a single-PIN map site and are prospects for the broker.
Market Partner Assigned one semi-exclusive Tsplits™ market, where they build a virtual network and can establish an unlimited number of Root Mapsites.
Protected (semi-exclusive) market “There is no direct competition, but neighbouring markets may from time to time place PINs that identify objects, projects or opportunities their neighbours may have under contract, legally.” That is reciprocation, not dilution.
Home PIN The pin that claims a market. A Home PIN market typically includes 50 miles (USA) or 80 km (Canada) around a population centre. Smaller centres make the best market anchors; a centre of over 100,000 people is not suitable.
PIN Place of Interest Node: a marker on the map that opens an image, write-up and links when clicked. Essentially a listing.
Service floor The minimum commission and minimum listing term the broker sets. Buyers and sellers who want their listing on the map site pay at least that.
SPLITS Revenues are split between Corporate, Market Partners and Root Account Partners.
Virtual markets Ralf’s term for the self-contained local network a map site creates: listings plus local businesses and service providers.
TEB Talis eBook: page-turning eBooks made from PDFs.
TTV Talis TV: optional audio and video production and online broadcasting, planned for late 2026 or early 2027.
TVA Talis Value Added: listing analysis and review of possible alternate deal structures or investor classes.
FAST Code Free Allocation - Standard Tracking: a temporary code generated with the map site, activated when the account is registered.
MLS® Multiple Listing Service. MLS® is a trademark owned by The Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA).
